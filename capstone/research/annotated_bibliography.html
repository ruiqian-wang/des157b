1. Kress, Tricia M., and Robert Lake. “Living the Dystopian-Utopian Tension as Praxis: Transformative Dreaming With/In/for Education and Educational Research.” Educational Philosophy and Theory, vol. 52, no. 9, 27 Jan. 2020, pp. 931–936, https://doi.org/10.1080/00131857.2020.1716451.

In this paper, Papastephanou argues that the tension between utopian and dystopian visions should not be dismissed but rather embraced as a productive force in educational thought. The author introduces the concept of “transformative dreaming,” suggesting that imagined futures—both hopeful and cautionary—can inspire critical engagement with the present and provoke educational change. This source is relevant to my project as it supports the integration of speculative design methods and narrative futures in shaping interactive learning experiences. Papastephanou concludes that the act of imagining alternatives is not escapist, but essential to disrupting normative assumptions in education. I find this framework both grounding and inspirational, and it encourages me to design with both idealism and realism, using imagined futures as tools for practical reflection and decision-making.

2. Innovare. “Why Interactive Data Visualization Is the Key to Better Student Outcomes.” Innovare | Social Innovation Partners, 4 Dec. 2023, innovaresip.com/blog/interactive-data-visualization-in-education-guide/. Accessed 15 May 2025.

This article shows the transformative role of interactive data visualization in education. It discusses how dynamic dashboards, heat maps, time-series graphs, interactive geographic mapping, and decision-making trees can help educators identify patterns, monitor progress, and make informed decisions to enhance student learning outcomes. It underscores the importance of real-time data interaction, which enables educators to adapt teaching strategies and interventions based on current student performance.

For my project, this resource provides concrete examples of how interactive visual tools can be used to create engaging, data-driven educational experiences. This reinforces my project’s objective to utilize interactive design elements to visualize future learning scenarios compellingly.

3. Fitzpatrick, Dan. “3 Reasons Why Education Urgently Needs Speculative Design.” Forbes, 26 July 2024, www.forbes.com/sites/danfitzpatrick/2024/07/26/3-reasons-why-education-urgently-needs-speculative-design/.

In the article, Dan Fitzpatrick advocates for the integration of speculative design into educational practices to better prepare students for an unpredictable future shaped by rapid technological advancements. He outlines three primary reasons for this need: fostering adaptability in an ever-changing world, making abstract future concepts tangible through immersive experiences, and reimagining the foundational structures of education. Fitzpatrick emphasizes that speculative design encourages critical thinking by allowing students and educators to explore “what if” scenarios, thereby promoting proactive engagement with potential future challenges. This perspective reinforces the value of incorporating speculative methodologies into the design projects. ￼
